History demonstrates that man's progress is dynamic and logarithmic,
not static or linear. Look at the historical, logarithmic chart on the U.S.
stock market dating back to 1789 (see Figure 2, courtesy Foundation for
the Study of Cycles). Sometimes advances occur in spurs, followed by
consolidation phases that last for long periods of time. Then again,
occasionally the reverse happens. This reveals the market's dynamic
behavior. The relatively consistent advance on a log scale for the last 200-
plus years demonstrates the logarithmic nature of economic progress.
