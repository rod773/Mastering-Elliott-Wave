This is done because X-waves can be virtually any Corrective pattem
without effecting the name or overall look of the larger formation. An X-
wave can even be a Non-Standard pattern when the time period covered by
the two surrounding patterns is long enough to allow for it. When
reviewing the listing in Table B (page 8-11), keep in mind that an X-wave
will usually alternate its formation with the Corrective pattem which
immediately preceded it. For example, if the first Correction is a Zigzag,
the x-wave will probably be a monowave, Flat or Triangle. If the first
Correction is a Flat, the X-wave will probably be a monowave, Zigzag or
Non-Standard formation (probably not a Triangle). Exceptions do occur,
but they are relatively rare.
